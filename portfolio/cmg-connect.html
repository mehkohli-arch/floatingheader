CMG Connect: Realtor App

Testing a new app for real estate agents before launch, and finding where people got stuck so the team could fix it early.

The situation

A new app for real estate agents

CMG Financial is a national mortgage lender that works with a large network of real estate agents, who use technology to manage their clients and loans.

Agents had been using a third-party app that often caused confusion and slowed them down. CMG built its own replacement, CMG Connect, a web app that works on phones and computers, to make agents' work simpler and give their clients a more consistent experience.

Before launch, we tested a prototype with real agents to find problems early.

What is usability testing? Watching real people try real tasks with a design, to see where it works and where they get stuck, before anything is built.

The problem

If it confuses agents, they'll leave

Agents needed a tool they could learn quickly and use with confidence. The risks were:

  • A confusing sign-up that makes people give up.
  • Misunderstanding how the app is organized, for example the difference between leads (potential clients) and loans (clients already in progress).
  • Losing trust in the new tool if it didn't match how agents think about their work.

We needed to test real tasks: signing up, inviting clients, tracking a loan's progress, uploading documents and sending invites.

My role

UX research lead

Led the research

Planned and ran both live and self-guided usability tests.

Mapped the tasks

Created detailed step-by-step flows for each key task agents would do.

Made sense of results

Turned observations into clear insights and problem statements.

Recommended changes

Delivered research-backed fixes for navigation, labels and clarity.

How I approached it

Real tasks, real people

  1. Turn real work into test tasks

    I mapped the full journey: signing up, inviting clients, tracking loans, uploading documents and sending invites. Then I broke it into scenarios that match how an agent strings tasks together in a real day.

    Each scenario was scripted to guide people while still leaving room to explore naturally.

  1. Build a clickable prototype

    I built an interactive prototype focused on how the app is organized and how people move through it, not final visual polish.

    I made sure every key action worked (uploading, switching between leads and loans, sending invites) so gaps wouldn't skew the results.

  1. Run two kinds of tests

    Between January 4 and 16, 2024, I ran live sessions to observe closely and ask follow-up questions, and self-guided sessions to see how agents use it on their own. Participants were real estate agents across the US, ages 26 to 64, recruited through the testing platform Userlytics.

    For the self-guided tests, the prototype and instructions had to stand on their own, so I built the task prompts right into the test.

  1. Measure and find patterns

    I tracked success or failure at every step to see exactly where people dropped off.

    Then I grouped everything people said and did into themes (called affinity mapping) to spot patterns across testers.

What we found

What worked, and what didn't

Worked

Fast start

Agents set up their profile and sent their first invite in under 10 minutes.

Worked

Invites were smooth

Sending invites was easy, though every agent wanted to preview the invite first.

Problem

"Leads" vs. "loans" confusion

The difference wasn't clear, and tabs labeled "Active" and "Preapproved" made it worse. Many agents failed here.

Problem

Two ways to upload documents

Uploads lived under both "View Details" and "Tasks." Only 4 of 5 agents finished without frustration.

Worked

Simple and complete

Every agent praised how simple it was, nothing felt missing, and they loved seeing their loan's progress step by step.

Ideas

What agents asked for

Saved house lists, better calculators, email integration and more personalization.

Overall: 60%+ of testers failed or got confused in navigation and labeling, even though the overall layout felt intuitive.

What I recommended

  • Combine "Leads" and "Loans" into a single "Customers" page, using words agents already use.
  • Make uploading obvious: clear upload buttons, rename "Tasks," and add short pop-up tips.
Challenges

Working fast, testing well

Little time for live sessions

The project moved fast, so detailed flows and scenarios guided the self-guided testers.

Making it feel real

Tasks like uploading documents had to feel functional, even with no real data behind the prototype.

Scope vs. depth

We couldn't test everything, so I focused on the tasks that matter most to the business.

Detail vs. speed

Realistic enough for meaningful feedback, but simple enough to change quickly.

Results

What it delivered

<10 min

Quick to get started

Sign-up and the first client invite took under 10 minutes.

100%

Core features validated

Every tester named simplicity and progress tracking as strengths.

~30%

Less support needed

Projected drop in support requests and training time from the recommended changes.

The app is still in progress, and this research is already shaping the next round of designs.

What I learned

Words matter as much as layout

Even simple-looking flows can hide confusion, especially when the labels use internal language (like "lead" vs. "loan") that doesn't match how people think.

By focusing on core tasks and realistic scenarios, I found and prioritized the problems before development began, so CMG Connect can launch with clearer design, fewer support requests and more trust from agents.
